WHAT TO SAY

– How to have the conversation with clarity, firmness, and respect.
SUGGESTED OPENING
“You are highly valuable to this organization, and I want you here. But your interactions with the leadership team are now affecting performance and trust. We have discussed this before, and today I need us to establish exactly what must change, how we will measure it, and what will happen next.”
IF THEY...
LIKELY RESPONSE HOW TO REPLY
Get Defensive
I understand this is difficult to hear. My intent is not to criticize you, but to resolve an issue that is impacting the team and our results.
Agree Quickly
Thank you. Let’s make sure we are aligned on what will change, how we will measure it, and when we will review progress.
Blame Others
I hear your perspective. Regardless of cause, the impact is real, and we need to focus on what you will do differently starting now.
Minimize the Issue
I understand it may not feel serious, otherwise the data and feedback show meaningful change is needed.
WHAT NOT TO SAY
Don’t argue about the past.
Stay focused on the facts and the future.
Don’t label the person.
Avoid character judgments or fixed identities.
Don’t threaten impulsively.
Consequences must be clear, measured, and fair.
Don’t over-explain.
Keep it simple, concise, and direct.
Don’t make it personal.
Focus on behavior and impact, not personality.
CONVERSATION OBJECTIVES
State the concern clearly
Define measurable expectations
Confirm the review date
Protect dignity while holding standards
Listen intently to words and body language
TONE TO MAINTAIN
Calm
Lead with composure, not emotion.
Direct
Be clear and straightforward.
Respectful
Value the person while addressing the issue.
Firm
Hold the standard with consistency.
Lead the conversation with clarity, not emotion.
Create emails, letters, memos, or other documentation to follow up and communicate effectively.
